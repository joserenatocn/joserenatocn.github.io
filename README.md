# The Bradbury Challenge

A public log of a year of short stories and a thousand nights of reading.

**→ [Read the log](https://yourusername.github.io)**

---

## What this is

In 2001, Ray Bradbury gave the keynote at Point Loma Nazarene University's Writer's Symposium by the Sea, a talk called *Telling the Truth*. His advice to young writers was simple, and a little outrageous:

- **Don't start with novels.** They take too long. Write short stories instead, about one a week. By the end of the year you'll have fifty-two, and Bradbury's bet was that nobody can write fifty-two bad ones in a row.
- **Fill your head.** Every night for a thousand nights, before bed, read one short story, one poem, and one essay. Choose the essays from every field: science, history, philosophy, anything.

This repository is my attempt at both.

## The rules (as I'm playing them)

**Writing: 52 stories in a year**

- One complete story a week. Doesn't matter if they are any good.

**Reading: 1,000 nights**

1. One short story
2. One poem
3. One essay

I'll try to be as strict as possible within the constraints of, well, life. But this should be a challenge of enjoyment, growth and discovery, not dread, pressure and demotivation. A missed night or a missed week doesn't end it. I just pick it back up.

## What I hope to get out of it

- **The habit.** Writing every week, whatever the week looks like.
- **Finishing things.** Fifty-two beginnings, middles, and endings, instead of one novel stuck at chapter four.
- **Craft at the sentence level.** In a short story every sentence has to earn its place.
- **Range of voice.** Learning which narrators come naturally to me and which ones I have to fight for.
- **Testing ideas.** Some stories will take place in worlds I hope to write novels in. The short form is a cheap way to find out whether an idea is a story, a novella, or a book.
- **A few stories worth sending out.** Collecting rejections is part of the work.

## Why I won't post my stories (at least initially)

Most magazines consider a story published once it appears anywhere online, including a personal site. So this log records the process: the title, length, narrator stance, what I was practicing, and what I learned. The stories themselves wills tay in a drawer (at least initially).

Built with Jekyll and hosted free on GitHub Pages.

- **New week:** Add file → Create new file → name it `_stories/week-01.md` (then `week-02.md`, …) → paste in `_templates/new-week.md` → fill it in → Commit.
- **Reading log:** edit `_data/reading.yml`.
- **Title, name, goal:** edit `_config.yml`.
- **Design:** edit `assets/css/style.css`.
